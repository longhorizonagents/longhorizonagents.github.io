Overview

Long-horizon performance is the new dominant axis.

Frontier LLM-based agents — Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, OpenHands, SWE-agent, MetaGPT — are now deployed on tasks that span hours of execution, hundreds of thousands of tokens, and dozens of tool calls. Yet they degrade sharply as the horizon grows: effective context shrinks under noise, agents cannot accumulate and reuse experience across sessions, and alignment guarantees that hold for single-turn chat erode under extended autonomy.

The 1st Workshop on Long-Horizon Agents treats this as one inseparable problem rather than separate silos, around three emphases: Context Management — memory, retrieval, compression, and context engineering that keep agents coherent over millions of tokens; Agent Evolution — self-improvement, skill acquisition, lifelong learning, and online RL on agent traces; and Safe AI — alignment, monitoring, control, and evaluation that remain reliable under unsupervised execution.

To our knowledge, the only workshop positioning long-horizon execution × context engineering × on-the-fly evolution × safety-under-extended-autonomy as a single, inseparable intersection.

Why now. 2025–2026 marked the transition of long-horizon agents from research demos to deployed products. METR reports frontier-model capability doubling roughly every seven months on multi-hour tasks, and benchmarks such as SWE-bench, GAIA, OSWorld, and RE-Bench have largely collapsed under agentic scaffolding. A NeurIPS-level integrative venue is needed now, before the next generation of long-horizon agents is re-invented in each silo.

Call for Papers

Submit your work.

We invite empirical, theoretical, systems, position, and survey papers across context & memory, agent evolution, long-horizon planning & reasoning, multi-agent orchestration, tool/code/computer use, evaluation & benchmarks, safety under extended autonomy, and open infrastructure.

Two tracks in the NeurIPS 2026 format — short (up to 4 pages) and long (up to 8 pages), plus references and appendix. The workshop is non-archival; all accepted papers are presented as posters, with ~6 selected for short oral talks and 1–2 for best-paper recognition. Reviewing is on OpenReview with three reviews per paper; we disallow work already published at or concurrent with major ML/NLP venues, and encourage releasing code, data, traces, and harnesses.

OpenReview → Submission portal opens July 18, 2026.
