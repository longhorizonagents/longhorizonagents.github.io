01 Overview

Long-horizon performance is the new dominant axis.

Frontier LLM-based agents — Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, OpenHands, SWE-agent, MetaGPT — are now deployed on tasks that span hours of execution, hundreds of thousands of tokens, and dozens of tool calls. Yet they degrade sharply as the horizon grows: effective context shrinks under noise, agents cannot accumulate and reuse experience across sessions, and alignment guarantees that hold for single-turn chat erode under extended autonomy.

Long-horizon performance now cuts across capability, infrastructure, and safety in a way no single existing community addresses end-to-end. The 1st Workshop on Long-Horizon Agents brings together researchers from foundation-model training, reinforcement learning, agent systems, and AI safety around three representative emphases.

i

Context Management

Memory, retrieval, compression, and context engineering that keep agents coherent over millions of tokens.

ii

Agent Evolution

Self-improvement, skill acquisition, lifelong learning, and online RL on agent traces.

iii

Safe AI

Alignment, monitoring, control, and evaluation that remain reliable under unsupervised execution.

To our knowledge, the only workshop positioning long-horizon execution × context engineering × on-the-fly evolution × safety-under-extended-autonomy as a single, inseparable intersection.

↗ Why now

2025–2026 marked the transition of long-horizon agents from research demos to deployed products. METR reports frontier-model capability doubling roughly every seven months on multi-hour tasks, and benchmarks such as SWE-bench, GAIA, OSWorld, and RE-Bench have largely collapsed under agentic scaffolding. A NeurIPS-level integrative venue is needed now, before the next generation of long-horizon agents is re-invented in each silo.

02 Topics

What we invite.

We welcome empirical, theoretical, systems, position, and survey papers across the long-horizon agent agenda. Suggested topics (non-exhaustive):

  1. 01

    Context & Memory

    Long-context architectures, retrieval-augmented agents, episodic / semantic memory, context compression and engineering, lost-in-the-middle mitigations, virtual-memory schemes.

  2. 02

    Agent Evolution

    Self-improvement, lifelong learning, skill libraries, online RL on agent traces, recursive self-improvement, automatic workflow synthesis.

  3. 03

    Long-Horizon Planning & Reasoning

    Hierarchical decomposition, search and backtracking, plan repair, deliberate vs. reactive control, test-time compute scaling.

  4. 04

    Multi-Agent Systems & Orchestration

    Role specialization, communication protocols, optimizable agent graphs, debate, market and contract mechanisms.

  5. 05

    Tool Use, Code & Computer Use

    Robust tool calling, environment grounding, sandboxing, SWE and computer-use agents, scientific-discovery agents.

  6. 06

    Evaluation & Benchmarks

    Long-horizon and multi-day tasks, human-comparable horizon measurement, agent-as-judge, reproducibility and contamination, cost–latency–reliability frontiers.

  7. 07

    Safety Under Extended Autonomy

    Reward hacking on long trajectories, scheming and deceptive alignment, AI control protocols, scalable oversight, runtime monitoring of agent traces.

  8. 08

    Open Infrastructure

    Agent traces, harnesses, simulators, datasets, and open-source frameworks enabling reproducible long-horizon research.

08 Call for Papers

Submit your work.

Two tracks in the NeurIPS 2026 proceedings format. The workshop is non-archival; accepted papers are posted on the workshop site.

Track A

Short paper

up to 4 pages + references / appendix

For focused empirical results, position pieces, and early-stage ideas.

Track B

Long paper

up to 8 pages + references / appendix

For complete studies, systems, surveys, and theoretical contributions.

Presentation

All accepted papers are presented as posters; ~6 are selected for short oral talks and 1–2 for best-paper recognition.

Reviewing

OpenReview, 3 reviews per paper, double-blind, with a program committee sized to ≥3× expected submissions.

Originality

The CFP disallows work already published at NeurIPS / ICML / ICLR or other ML / NLP venues, and work concurrent with the main NeurIPS 2026 conference.

Openness

We encourage the release of code, data, traces, or harnesses. Best Paper & Best Poster awards carry monetary prizes.

OpenReview → Submission portal opens July 18, 2026.
