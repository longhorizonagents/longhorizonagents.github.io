Overview

Long-horizon execution is the axis agents are now judged on.

Frontier LLM-based agents — Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, OpenHands, SWE-agent, MetaGPT — are moving from short conversations to bounded tasks that occupy hours or days: maintaining a codebase, conducting a literature review, running experiments, auditing a system, or coordinating a multi-step workflow. These are single extended episodes with a concrete goal, evolving intermediate artifacts, many tool calls, occasional human input, and a final deliverable that must be accepted or rejected.

The 1st Workshop on Long-Horizon Agents treats this as one inseparable problem rather than separate silos, around three coupled emphases: Context — working memory, retrieval, compression, artifact tracking, checkpointing, and context engineering that preserve task state over long trajectories; Interaction — human-in-the-loop clarification, tool use, environment interaction, agent–agent communication, interruption, handoff, and recovery; and Reflection & Evaluation — milestone verification, trajectory auditing, agent-as-judge, process supervision, self-repair, and reward densification when the success signal is sparse. Across all three runs a focus on cost efficiency — trading off accuracy, latency, token budget, tool cost, and human supervision.

To our knowledge, the only workshop positioning bounded long-horizon task execution × context × interaction × reflection/evaluation × cost efficiency as a single agenda.

Why now. 2025–2026 marked the transition of long-horizon agents from research demos to deployed products. METR reports frontier-model capability doubling roughly every seven months on multi-hour tasks, and newer benchmarks — ProgramBench, NL2Repo-Bench, Terminal-Bench, ClawBench, APEX-Agents, SkillsBench — push toward sustained, days-long execution. Yet evaluation still tends to score final answers and short rollouts, leaving open what should be remembered, when an agent should ask for help, how intermediate progress should be judged, and how cost should be reported. A NeurIPS-level integrative venue is needed now, before long-horizon agents are compared with incompatible private traces and cost claims.

Call for Papers

Submit your work.

We invite empirical, theoretical, systems, position, and survey papers across context for task state (working / artifact memory, retrieval, compression, checkpointing), interaction during execution (human clarification, interrupt protocols, tool / API use, agent delegation, recovery), reflection, verification & evaluation (milestone design, dense rewards, process supervision, agent-as-judge, trajectory auditing), planning, control & cost (decomposition, plan repair, latency–cost–reliability tradeoffs), and domains, safety & infrastructure (SWE / code, web, scientific, enterprise, embodied agents; trace monitors, datasets, harnesses, verifiers).

Two non-archival tracks in the NeurIPS 2026 format — short (up to 4 pages) and long (up to 8 pages), plus references and appendix. All accepted papers are presented as posters, with ~6 selected for short oral talks and 1–2 for best-paper recognition. Reviewing is on OpenReview with three reviews per paper; we disallow work already published at or concurrent with major ML/NLP venues, and encourage releasing code, data, traces, and harnesses.

OpenReview → Submission portal opens July 18, 2026.
